S1

Greyscale Photo Split

Grayscale lifestyle photo, slanted charcoal panel, thin orange stripe, one piece of real product proof.

MoodProfessional · Mobile · Real people
BackgroundsCharcoal panel + grayscale photo
ImageryGrayscale photography, optional selective-orange garment

What it is

A charcoal text panel with a slanted edge and a thin orange stripe sits beside a grayscale photo of a real person. One piece of product proof (a real app screen, the “You have a new mail item.” card, or an icon badge) shows how Anytime Mailbox fits into their day.

The idea behind it

People living flexible, on-the-go lives, with their mail quietly handled. The slant adds forward motion, the photo shows the “anywhere,” and the product proof shows the “how.” It is the Option A half of the drip pair.

How it ties to the brand

It is the brand’s own imagery rule (grayscale photography with an orange accent) turned into a layout. Orange is saved for the one phrase that matters and the CTA, so it stays the hero accent. Real app UI keeps every claim honest: we show what the product does instead of describing it.

Signature elements

  • Slanted charcoal panel (layout L01), wide enough that every line clears the slant
  • 10–26px orange stripe along the slant
  • Grayscale photo, subject centered in the visible area
  • Exactly one proof element: app phone, notification card, icon badge, Q&A chips or gift card
  • Orange CTA pill with a soft orange glow
  • Roboto Black headline, one orange phrase

Use it for

  • Email hero banners, referral images and 700×175 strips
  • Meta and LinkedIn ads about lifestyle, travel, remote work or professionalism
  • Newsletter feature blocks, decks, one-pagers and eBook covers
  • Any time you have a licensed photo that matches the audience

Skip it when

  • No photo fits the message (use S2)
  • Step-by-step or abstract ideas that need drawing (use S2)
  • Feeds where you need humor to stop the scroll (use S9)

S2

Illustrated

Flat line illustrations with charcoal outlines on cream, orange or charcoal, with dot grids and confetti.

MoodFriendly · Simple · Upbeat
BackgroundsCream #FFF5EF, orange or charcoal + dot grid
ImageryCode-drawn flat illustration (mailbox, envelope, phone, mug…)

What it is

Flat, friendly illustrations with 6–8px charcoal outlines on cream, orange or charcoal backgrounds. A smiling mailbox, envelopes, a phone or a coffee mug carries the idea, with a subtle dot grid and a few sparkles or confetti. It is the Option B half of the drip pair.

The idea behind it

Turn the product into a small cast of friendly objects so steps and benefits feel easy. It explains things photos can’t: three-step flows, “almost open for business,” an inbox on your phone.

How it ties to the brand

It uses only the brand palette (the orange family, charcoal and cream), Roboto Black headlines with one orange phrase, and the flat, minimal icon language from the brand guidelines. The “pop” CTA with a hard offset shadow adds energy without adding a color.

Signature elements

  • 6–8px charcoal outlines with round joins (about 3.5px on strips)
  • Fills: orange, orange dark #CC5200, orange soft #FFDFC9, white
  • One hero object, centered, plus small accents
  • Numbered step badges joined by a dotted path
  • Dot grid at 10–18% opacity on the art side
  • “Pop” CTA: orange fill, charcoal border, hard offset shadow

Use it for

  • Onboarding, welcome and how-it-works messages
  • Step flows and carousels (the path can run across tiles)
  • Friendlier versions of drip banners when no photo fits
  • eBooks and one-pagers that explain a process

Skip it when

  • Premium or corporate B2B pieces where real people build trust (use S1 or S3)
  • Security warnings (S10 has its own visual world)
  • Ads that need a photo of the target audience

S3

Clean

One sharp hook, one orange phrase, one proof element: native-feeling ads on white, charcoal or orange.

MoodClear · Native · Useful
BackgroundsWhite, #F8F9FA, charcoal or full orange
ImageryGrayscale photo or a real UI prop (toggle, alert, checklist, app screen)

What it is

Clean, native-feeling feed ads. A Roboto Black hook with one orange phrase, a grayscale photo or a real UI prop (an out-of-office toggle, the notification card, a typographic checklist, an angled app screen) and an orange pill CTA.

The idea behind it

Read like a useful post, not an ad. Reddit and feed audiences reward clarity, so each piece makes one point with one proof element. A “TL;DR” or a checklist does the selling by being easy to understand.

How it ties to the brand

It is the brand voice (clear, benefit-driven, no hype) made visual: charcoal instead of black, grayscale photos with an orange accent, the official logo at a fixed position and spacing, and Roboto only.

Signature elements

  • Logo top-left at 64/64; exactly 72px (4:5) or 60px (1:1) from logo to headline caps
  • Headline Roboto Black 88–104px, tracking −0.025em, line-height 0.98
  • UI props: toggle chip, alert card, “in a box” struck through → “online ✓”
  • Orange pill CTA ~40px text
  • Nothing smaller than ~38px on a 1080 canvas

Use it for

  • Reddit and Meta conversion ads
  • Carousels that summarize (TL;DR, checklists)
  • Newsletter blocks and B2B email images that make one practical point
  • Practical-guide eBooks

Skip it when

  • Concepts that need humor or spectacle (use S9)
  • Illustrated step flows (use S2)
  • Pieces with more than one message

S4a

Maki: Cinematic Dark

Low-key grayscale photography, an orange signal bar that bleeds off the left edge, and white type that reads like a film still.

Based on Maki’s campaign carousels (shipping label, stolen envelope, expat mail), refined to brand.

MoodCinematic · Serious · Personal
BackgroundsFull-bleed low-key photo + charcoal gradient (#1A1D20)
ImageryGrayscale night or low-light photography at ~60% brightness; optional orange duotone for travel

What it is

A full-bleed, low-key grayscale photo fills the frame, with a charcoal gradient where the type sits. The key line of the headline is set in white on an orange bar that bleeds off the left edge (the signal bar). Supporting copy is white with bold-white emphasis beside a short orange edge tab. Carousels add a white circle swipe cue.

The idea behind it

Make the stakes feel real without fear imagery. Night streets, a stack of envelopes, someone checking a phone after dark: everyday moments where mail and privacy matter. The bar works like a highlighter on the one line we want remembered.

How it ties to the brand

Our imagery rule is grayscale photography with an orange accent, so Maki’s color photos are converted to grayscale and darkened. That keeps the mood and leaves orange as the only color. Charcoal (never black), Roboto Black and the official white lockup keep it unmistakably ATMB.

Signature elements

  • Full-bleed grayscale photo at ~60% brightness, subject clear of the type
  • Signal bar: white Roboto Black on an orange bar that bleeds off the left edge (max two bar lines)
  • A plain white setup line above the bar (“Living abroad?”)
  • 16–18px orange edge tab beside body copy
  • White circle swipe cue bottom-right on carousel tiles (never on the last tile)
  • Optional proof: notification card or app phone with a soft orange glow

Use it for

  • Privacy, security and “keep your home address private” stories
  • Travel, expat and remote-work lifestyle posts
  • Story-led carousels and email heroes
  • SecuriTIP variants about scams and phishing

Skip it when

  • Light, cheerful onboarding (use S2)
  • Photos where the face is already in shadow
  • Copy-heavy or step-by-step pieces (use S3 or S6)

Improved from the originals

  • Color photos converted to grayscale and darkened to match the brand imagery rule
  • One signal-bar system: bars always bleed from the left edge with the same padding and gap, max two bar lines
  • Type sizes locked per format (bar 82–96px on 1080 canvases, body ≥38px)
  • Logo fixed at top-left 64/64 instead of moving between corners
  • Final carousel tile now carries a plain-text CTA; the empty trailing page is gone
  • Third-party statistics left out unless they are sourced and approved

Maki: Orange Cutout

Orange duotone photo backgrounds with full-color cutout people, white type beside a light vertical rule, and hand-drawn white marks.

Based on Maki’s expat mail carousel, refined to brand.

MoodWarm · Friendly · Personal
BackgroundsFull-bleed orange duotone photo of a place, with a deep-orange gradient behind text
ImageryFull-color cutout people with a white sticker outline, real app screens, envelope cutouts

What it is

A full-bleed photo of a place (a street, a café, a city at dusk) becomes an orange duotone, so the whole canvas reads as brand orange. Full-color cutout people with white sticker outlines stand in front, often holding a phone, joined by real app screens and hand-drawn white “!!!” marks. Type is white: Roboto Black headlines beside a light peach vertical rule, short white body copy, and an orange swipe cue with a white ring on carousels.

The idea behind it

Make the expat and travel story feel warm and personal. Orange sets the mood before anyone reads a word, and the cutout people bring the human, “that’s me” moment that Maki’s expat carousel did well.

How it ties to the brand

Orange is the background, so the brand color does the heavy lifting; white and charcoal are the only type colors, Roboto runs throughout, and proof comes from real app screens. Full-color cutouts are a deliberate exception to the grayscale photo rule, limited to the people and props in front of the orange.

Signature elements

  • Orange duotone background: grayscale photo multiplied over #FF6600 with a light orange wash
  • Deep-orange gradient behind the text for contrast
  • White Roboto Black headline beside a 6px peach (#FFDFC9) vertical rule
  • Full-color cutout people with a 12–15px white outline and warm shadow, bleeding off an edge
  • Hand-drawn white “!!!” marks near a head or prop
  • Real app screens (the action menu) and envelope cutouts as proof
  • Orange swipe cue with a white ring and white arrow (carousels only)
  • White pill CTA on orange for ads, email and documents

Use it for

  • Expat, travel and digital-nomad stories
  • Friendly carousels and story-style posts
  • Email heroes and one-pagers for international audiences
  • Lighter SecuriTIP topics

Skip it when

  • Serious privacy or security warnings (use S4a)
  • Backgrounds where people or faces would show through the duotone
  • Long copy on orange

Improved from the originals

  • Logo fixed at the top-left (it moved between corners)
  • Backgrounds are places, so no ghost faces show through the duotone
  • One vertical-rule and swipe-cue style across tiles
  • First-person “I used to rely on family…” copy rewritten in second person, so it can’t read as a testimonial
  • Final tile now carries a plain-text CTA

Paulo: Paper Cutout

Paper-texture collage: torn orange paper, black-and-white cutout people with white sticker outlines, orange halftone props and hand-drawn marks.

Based on Paulo’s small-business carousels (first 10 customers, the economy, AI side businesses, LLC privacy), refined to brand.

MoodEditorial · Hands-on · Optimistic
BackgroundsOff-white paper texture, torn orange paper sections, faint gray facets
ImageryB&W cutout people with a white sticker outline, orange halftone props, B&W photo strips

What it is

An off-white paper background with a torn sheet of orange paper (top, bottom or side). Black-and-white cutout people with white sticker outlines overlap the torn edge, joined by orange halftone props (phone, envelope, mailbox, laptop) and a few hand-drawn orange marks. Headlines are tight Roboto Black in charcoal with one orange line, a short orange rule, big orange “#1” numbers and an orange “Example” pill.

The idea behind it

Make small-business advice feel personal and handmade, like a founder’s mood board. Cutouts put real people front and center; halftone props and quick marker strokes add energy without adding new colors.

How it ties to the brand

Black-and-white people with orange props is our grayscale-plus-orange rule in collage form. Roboto Black headlines with one orange line, charcoal text and the orange pill CTA (ads, email, documents) keep it on-brand.

Signature elements

  • Paper texture background with faint gray facets
  • Torn orange paper band with a white fiber edge
  • B&W cutout people with a 12–15px white sticker outline and soft shadow
  • Orange halftone props: phone, envelope, mailbox, laptop, padlock
  • Hand-drawn orange marks: burst lines, plus signs, swirl
  • Roboto Black headline, one orange line, 7px orange rule under it
  • Big orange “#1” numbers and an orange “Example” pill
  • Orange vertical bar beside supporting copy
  • Orange filled-circle swipe cue on carousels

Use it for

  • Small-business tips, growth and “look established” content
  • Numbered-tip carousels
  • Founder and side-business newsletters and email heroes

Adding a new style (design pegs)

A design peg is a reference to try on the brand: a screenshot, a Dribbble or Pinterest link, a competitor ad, a Canva template. Here is how it becomes a style in this guide.

  1. Read the peg. Break down its layout, type, color, imagery, devices and mood.
  2. Translate it. Orange stays the hero accent; Roboto does most of the work; borrow ideas, never artwork or another brand’s look.
  3. Confirm the sample plan. Formats, real ATMB copy, imagery source, depth.
  4. Build the kit. Social sizes, carousel, newsletter, Reddit, email drip, SecuriTIP, slides, PDF, eBook.
  5. Review. Shared with a peg board contact sheet for review.
  6. Decide. Approved (added here as S7, S8…), Trial, or Retired with the reason logged.

Glossary

Slant panel
The charcoal text area with an angled edge that defines S1 (layout L01).
Stripe
The thin orange band that runs along the slant.
Proof element
Real product evidence: an app screen, the “You have a new mail item.” card, an icon badge or gift card. One per piece.
Selective color
Keeping one orange garment in color on a grayscale photo. Whole garment, clean edges, or none at all.
Pop CTA
S2’s button: orange fill, charcoal border and a hard offset shadow.
Hero object
The single main illustration in an S2 piece (mailbox, storefront, phone…).
Dot grid
The faint dot pattern behind S2 art (10–18% opacity).
Safe zone
The part of a 9:16 frame that app UI won’t cover: between 14% and 65% of the height.
Display face
A decorative Google font used only for an S9 hook (Anton, Alfa Slab One, Permanent Marker…).
Design peg
A reference brought in to try on the brand. It becomes a Trial style, then Approved or Retired.
Option A / B
The S1 (photo) and S2 (illustrated) versions of the same drip message, made for testing.
Overview sheet
The _Overview_…png contact sheet saved with every delivery folder.
Signal bar
S4’s white headline line on an orange bar that bleeds off the left edge.
Edge tab
S4’s short orange bar beside body copy, set against the left edge.
Sticker cutout
S5’s black-and-white person (or prop) cut from its background with a white outline.
Halftone prop
S5’s orange-dot version of a phone, envelope, mailbox, laptop or padlock.
Torn edge
The ripped-paper border between S5’s orange paper and the paper background.
Example callout
S6’s thin orange rule followed by an italic “Example:” line.
Icon tab
S6’s folded orange corner flag with a charcoal icon box.
Sub-style
A variant inside one style family (S4a, S4b, S4c) that shares its type and layout logic but changes the mood.
Orange duotone
S4b’s background: a grayscale photo multiplied over brand orange, so the whole canvas reads orange.
Orange spotlight
S11a’s single orange element that carries a blog heading’s idea: a disc, a device, an object or a divider.
Spotlight badge
S11a’s 124px orange disc with a white line icon. Two per blog image at most, always in empty space.
Clay render
S11a’s Concept 3D look: light-gray 3D objects with soft shadows and one orange hero object.
Gradient map
S11b’s duotone: background brightness mapped onto #CC5200 → #FF6600 → #FFB27F, so the background centers on the brand orange.
Seamless recolor
S11c’s way of making an object orange: the object keeps its own shading on an exact brand-orange ramp, so the lit tone is #FF6600, shadows go deeper and highlights stay.
Zoom QA
The check before any blog image ships: every quarter at 2× and every recolor or mask edge at 3–6×, next to the original.
Calmed color
S11c’s grade: saturation eased, blues and greens most, so the orange object reads first.
Shade map
S11d’s duotone: background brightness mapped by percentile onto shades and tints of #FF6600, so the median is exactly brand orange and the depth of S4b stays.
Eye-level background
A place photo shot from standing or sitting height, so it matches the cutout person’s viewpoint. Required for S11d.
Arch window
S7’s photo or illustration frame: a rounded-top arch whose radius is half its width.
Blob
S7’s soft organic shape in orange-soft or warm gray, made with an asymmetric border-radius.
Tile
One rounded compartment of an S8 grid: orange (headline), charcoal (feature, CTA, notification), cream, white or photo.
Accelerator streaks
S12’s tapered orange lines taken from the Business Accelerator logo: thick rounded head on the right, sharp tail on the left, always pointing into a title, illustration or number.
Topic tone
S12’s per-title color: Orange (Marketing & Growth), Charcoal (Team & Operations), Cream (Strategy & Planning) or Peach (Tech & Tools).
Webinar pair
An S12 deck and its matching recorded-video format (Ignite = D1 + V1, Daylight = D2 + V2).
Doodle marks
S11d’s 2–3 white hand-drawn strokes: burst ticks by the head plus a topic doodle (card, envelope, pin, shield, mailbox, checklist). Never letters or numbers.
